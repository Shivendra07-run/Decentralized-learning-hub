Three-Block Cryptographic Chain

Simulation

Edit the data inside any block. Notice how modifying even a single character breaks its cryptographic hash and invalidates every block downstream. Click "Mine" to find a valid Proof-of-Work nonce (difficulty: prefix "000") and repair the chain.

How It Works: Cryptographic Immutability & Nonces

Each block contains: (1) its own transaction data, (2) the cryptographic SHA-256 hash of the previous block, and (3) an arbitrary counter called a nonce. Because Block 2 includes Block 1's hash, changing Block 1 changes its resulting hash, which breaks Block 2's "Previous Hash" reference. Proof-of-Work requires nodes to spend compute power iterating nonces until the resulting hash satisfies a difficulty constraint (in this demo, starting with 000).

Smart Contract Vending Machine

Simulation

Select an item and insert mock coins. When you execute the contract, watch the code highlight in real-time as each line evaluates deterministically. If conditions are met, the item releases and change is refunded automatically.

How It Works: Autonomous Execution Without Intermediaries

A smart contract holds funds in its own address escrow. When triggered, it checks conditions (require(msg.value >= price)). If satisfied, it transfers property and refunds excess balances. If unsatisfied, the entire transaction reverts and all funds return to the sender. No human clerk or escrow attorney is involved.

Constant-Product AMM Swap (x · y = k)

Simulation

How It Works: The Invariant Curve and Price Impact

In an Automated Market Maker (AMM) like Uniswap v2, liquidity pools hold two tokens with reserves $x$ and $y$. Every trade preserves the constant product $k = x \cdot y$ (minus a 0.3% protocol fee). When you swap a large amount of Token A into the pool, you dramatically alter the reserve ratio, causing the marginal price of Token B to skyrocket—this is Price Impact.

How It Works: Staking Rewards & Compounding

In Proof-of-Stake protocols, validators lock up tokens as collateral to participate in transaction verification. In exchange for honest service and network security, the protocol awards freshly minted consensus rewards and tip fees. Reinvesting rewards periodically triggers exponential compound growth.

How It Works: Quorums, Plutocracy, and Governance

In a standard 1-token = 1-vote DAO, influence directly tracks financial capital rather than personhood. A proposal typically requires: (1) a Quorum (e.g. at least 50% of outstanding tokens must cast ballots), and (2) a Supermajority (e.g. over 60% of cast votes must approve). This simulation demonstrates why modern Web3 researchers study alternative mechanisms like Quadratic Voting and Delegation.

Why Token-Weighted vs. One-Wallet-One-Vote?

This live poll demonstrates a simple 1-wallet = 1-vote mechanism. In practice, permissionless networks cannot verify unique human identities without centralized KYC; a single actor could generate thousands of wallets for free (a Sybil attack) to manipulate the outcome. Real DAOs use token-weighted voting (governance proportional to skin in the game) or quadratic voting to resist manipulation.
