Frequently Asked Questions

Straightforward answers to the most common beginner dilemmas without marketing spin.

Is cryptocurrency safe for beginners? +

Cryptocurrency is built on battle-tested mathematics, but because it relies on true self-custody, you are personally responsible for protecting your keys. There are no password resets or bank branches. If you start small, practice on free testnets (like Sepolia), and never share your seed phrase, you can explore safely without financial risk.

Do I need real money to learn Web3? +

Absolutely not. All core concepts can be explored on free test networks (such as Sepolia or Polygon Amoy) using free testnet tokens from faucets, or in local simulated sandboxes like the Aether Interactive Lab. You should never risk real funds while learning.

What is a seed phrase and why is it so important? +

A seed phrase (usually 12 or 24 words) is a human-readable mathematical representation of your wallet's master private key. Anyone who learns these words can instantly steal all funds across every account derived from that wallet. Real applications and developers will never ask for your seed phrase.

Can someone reverse a blockchain transaction if I make a mistake? +

No. Blockchains are append-only cryptographic ledgers. Once a transaction is validated and included in a block, it is permanent and irreversible. The only way to retrieve sent funds is if the recipient voluntarily chooses to send them back.

What is the fundamental difference between Web2 and Web3? +

In Web2, centralized platforms own your account, host your data on private cloud servers, and control financial rails. In Web3, you log in using your cryptographic keypair, digital assets reside on public verifiable ledgers, and transactions run through open-source smart contracts without corporate intermediaries.

What happens if a company running a dApp goes out of business? +

Because smart contracts and digital assets are anchored directly on a decentralized blockchain rather than the company's private server, the logic and your token balances remain fully intact. Even if the company's web frontend shuts down, anyone can build a new interface or interact directly with the smart contract.

Why do blockchain transactions cost gas fees? +

Gas compensates independent validator nodes across the world for the compute cycles, bandwidth, and storage required to process and store your transaction. It also prevents malicious actors from flooding the network with denial-of-service loops.

How do hardware wallets protect private keys? +

A hardware wallet stores your private keys inside a physical, air-gapped Secure Element chip that never exposes the keys to your computer or internet browser. When you authorize a transaction, the hardware signs the payload internally and sends only the finished cryptographic signature back to the computer.
